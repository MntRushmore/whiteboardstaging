# Mathpix v3/text (SuperNet-201) as Agathon's crop reader

> Generated by `npm run eval:mpx-text` (`src/__eval__/mpx-text/**`; needs `RUN_MPX_TEXT_EVAL=1` and the Mathpix and OpenRouter keys); do not edit by hand
> outside the recommendation. Every line is the web scoreboards' synthetic ink (the tutor's hand engine in the four student
> hands; **no child's writing, no production data**), cropped exactly as an iPad's board crops it (`captureInkCrop`, the drawn
> path: black on white, ≤ 512 px, JPEG 0.8), and read by Mathpix `v3/text` (`recognizeImage`: `improve_mathpix: false`,
> auto-rotation off, `include_line_data`) and by today's crop reader, the recognize route's vision fallback (`google/gemini-3.1-flash-lite`,
> its prompt and body, the production privacy preferences). Mathpix's strokes reads are the scoreboard's, already on disk
> (no strokes call was made). Judged by the scoreboard's own `judgeRead`; stacked sums by `parseStacked`, long divisions by the
> bracket's divisor and dividend (the board's own parsers). Answers are cached under `src/__eval__/.cache/mpx-text/`.
> Run 2026-10-19; Mathpix answered as `SuperNet-201p1`. Calls: 369 v3/text + 5 v3/pdf pages (cap 400), 92 vision (cap 100).

## Recommendation

### 2026-10-19: put v3/text in front of the vision fallback (behind a flag), keep vision as the floor; use the two readers' disagreement, not a confidence, to ask the kid

**1. The crop reader: v3/text first, vision behind it — not a replacement.** On the same 85 crops v3/text read 74 right
against the vision model's 67, six times faster (p50 0.21 s against 1.29 s). The gap is the layouts the board parses:
stacked sums 10/10 against 4/10 (vision's six misses are the right digits in `\begin{array}{r@{\quad}l}`-style shapes the
board's stack parser rejects) and long division 20/20 against 8/10 (`3 ) 126`, no bracket). On the strokes misreads, the
lines a crop reader exists for, they tie: 13/24 against 15/24, each right where the other is wrong (either: 18/24).
Over every line with a strokes read, v3/text alone would be about as good as strokes (97.7 % against 97.1 %, estimated).
It is dearer per call ($0.002 against $0.00036), but a crop is read only after a strokes read failed, so the money is small.
Vision must stay behind it: v3/text is the same vendor, credentials and servers, so the outage the fallback exists for
(a credential failure flips the board to `vision`) takes v3/text down too. The prototype, `RECOGNIZE_CROP_READER=mathpix-text`
(off by default), does exactly that. It skips v3/text when the strokes failure says Mathpix is in trouble
(`auth`/`http`/`timeout`/`network`), gives it at most 2 s, and on any v3/text miss goes on to vision as today. v3/text said
`image_no_content` on a lone `5`, and vision reads those. Two gaps before turning it on. First, the board's crop retry
(`cropOnly`) follows any failed strokes read but does not say why. After a Mathpix timeout, v3/text can spend up to 2 s
before vision, which still has at least 3 s. Second, the case the flag is for, strokes Mathpix found **unreadable**
(`api_error`), is not in this corpus (0 on disk). Turn it on for a week and compare the crop retries' outcomes in the admin
console (provider, `unreadable`, retries), not the ink.

**2. A second opinion is where v3/text helps most, but not on every line yet.** Read each line's crop with v3/text in
parallel with strokes; the extra call (0.2 s) finishes before the strokes read does (0.45 s). Ask only when the two disagree:
by the estimate that asks about 2.7 % of lines and catches 71 % of strokes misreads, and a trusted read is right 99.1 % of
the time (97.1 % today). Adding "strokes confidence ≥ 0.99" asks about 4.5 % and catches 88 % (99.6 %). The cost is one more
Mathpix call on every line (it doubles Mathpix's cost per line) and a crop on every line, a client change. Swapping in
v3/text's read on a disagreement instead of asking fixes 13 of 24 misreads but breaks 1 % of right reads; today's second
reader (`/api/live/reread`, 73/100 real misreads fixed, 0/30 broken) is the better fixer. So the next step is to make
"v3/text disagrees" a **trigger** for the reread or the tap-to-fix prompt, measured on the misreads first. It is not a
reason to replace either.

**3. Confidence for "I read: … — tap to fix": v3/text's is the best single score here, but it is not good enough alone.**
SuperNet-201's new `text_confidence` / `layout_confidence` are **not returned for a line crop** (0 of 348: a crop is one `math`
region). They come only with a full page (`page_confidence` on v3/text, per region on v3/pdf), so the release adds nothing
to this question. The old `confidence` separates better than the strokes confidence: at ≥ 0.99 it asks about 2.5 % and
catches 71 % of wrong reads, where the strokes confidence asks about 3.6 % and catches 54 %. But the look-alikes every reader
shares come back at 0.95–1.00 (`a = 5` → `0=5`, `e^{x}` → `6^{x}`, `i` → `;`), and right long divisions at 0.52–0.89. The
vision model's confidence is no signal at all: it says 0.95 or 1.0 whatever it reads. If the flag is on, ask about a v3/text
crop read below 0.99. For the general prompt, the two readers' disagreement (item 2) beats any one threshold.

**4. Snap your homework → one problem per page: promising on clean pages, unproven on real ones.** On five synthetic
worksheets (34 problems: two columns, a 3 × 3 grid of column sums and long divisions, word problems with a table, two
"photos"), all three readers split every problem right with a simple geometric grouping. That grouping: a region that
starts with a number opens a problem, and every other region joins the nearest number above it in its column. The kid's
answers always landed with their problem. Use **v3/text with `include_line_data`**: 1.6 s a page, $0.002, against v3/pdf at
3.2 s and $0.005, which adds per-region text/layout confidence. Document layout gave nothing more. Three things the feature
must handle. Mathpix's reading order runs across columns (1, 6, 2, 7, …), so group by position, not order. Document layout
writes numbers as `\item[3.]`, and a long division's `6)` looks like a list number. And `is_handwritten` is almost never set on a
page's regions (1 of 279 across the three readers), so the kid's answer cannot be told from the printed problem by the flag.
Content can be wrong even when the split is right: the long divisions came back as `6) \frac{42}{252}`, the kid's quotient
read as a numerator, on all three readers (confidence 1.0 on v3/text, 0.83 on v3/pdf). Next: a few dozen real worksheet photos taken by the team (not kids' homework). A
real photo has a name on it, so keep `improve_mathpix: false` and store nothing.

## Caveats

- **None of this is a child's handwriting.** The lines are the tutor's own hand engine, perturbed; its glyphs are neat and
  font-like, and its `a`, `e`, `i` and `u` have look-alikes every reader trips on. The iPad reader study (feat/ipad-reader,
  docs/READER.md) reached the same limit. Real kids' ink decides it; this eval can only rank the readers on the same ink.
- The crop is the iPad's (the hand-drawn path of `captureInkCrop`). On desktop browsers the board's first try is tldraw's own
  export (variable-width, 2× pixel ratio), which may read differently.
- Strokes reads are the cached ones; young kids' lines, stacked sums and long divisions have none on disk, so those rows have
  no strokes comparison. No strokes call was made.
- The worksheets are clean, synthetic and well spaced. A real photo of homework (crowded, handwriting crossing problems, a
  page curling, a shadow over a column) is a much harder split; treat the worksheet result as "works on the easy case".
- Latency is from this Mac over home broadband; the server (Vercel, iad1) is likely faster to both providers.
