**Pick: keep `google/gemini-3.1-flash-lite` as the figure model, with `google/gemini-3.5-flash-lite` as its fallback** (`LIVE_MODELS.figure` / `figureFallback`; `LIVE_MODEL_FIGURE` overrides the primary).

- 3.1 Flash Lite is right on all 47 figures and writes no wrong answer, at 1.1 s p50 and under $0.001 a figure. Its structured read was used on 46 and was right on its own every time; the one it did not use (a right-angle box it wrote down as "90°", which is not on the figure) was answered by its own lines, which passed the sanity check.
- 3.5 Flash Lite is as fast and nearly as good (45/47), but misplaced two angles at a transversal and so wrote two wrong answers. It replaces Haiku 4.5 as the fallback: Haiku wrote 4 wrong answers of 47 at nearly three times the cost. Both are Google's (a US provider, as the owner prefers); OpenRouter serves each from more than one Google endpoint.
- GPT-5.4 nano is cheap but slow (3.6 s p50, 8.7 s p95) and misses 10 of 47.
- Against the old prompt on the same model (free-form lines, no checks): 44 → 47 right, 1 → 0 wrong, 2 → 0 with nothing written. The old prompt's wrong answer (parallel lines, 110 for 70) is the kind the structured read now catches twice over: the relation at a transversal comes from where each angle is, not from the name the model gives it (models placed the angles right more often than they named the relation), and any read that does not fit the labels or its own facts falls back or writes nothing.

What the corpus does not cover: labels misread by the recognizer (the labels here are sent as read right; a read whose labels disagree with the recognizer's is not used, but a misread both the recognizer and the model make is not caught), figures much messier than the corpus pen draws them, and several unknowns on one figure.
